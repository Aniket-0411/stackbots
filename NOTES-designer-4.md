## Rope facts measured in the engine (design rules I followed)
1. The rope is rigid and position-based, with no swing. A dangler hangs straight down at rope length from a grounded anchor.
2. An anchor holding a taut dangler CANNOT jump, because dampOutward kills its upward speed. Haul routes never need the anchor to jump.
3. Hauling: the dangler rises to about 18 px below the anchor's floor and is kept about one rope length away horizontally.
   So the anchor must walk on ground at least 1 tile higher than the lip, with at least L tiles of clear floor behind it.
   The lip must be at least 2 tiles wide. When the anchor steps back, the dangler drops onto the lip.
4. A single anchor cannot haul a chain of 2 or more danglers (tested). Every haul lifts one bot, and the next
   bot in the chain waits at the wall base so its rope stays slack.
5. A partner running ahead pulls a jumping bot back. Players have to move as a group, which is the point of the world.

## Questions / issues for engine and lead
- With the SPEC constants, a 3-tile gap at equal height needs an edge-perfect or coyote jump: flight about 91 px,
  but 96 px is needed to land a 24 px body past a 3-tile gap. SPEC §3.1 says gaps of 3 or less are jumpable. I used 2-wide pits
  or lower landings. Other worlds' 3-gaps may feel pixel-precise.
- Rope behaviour is half/half only when both bots have the same grounded state; otherwise the anchor logic in pullToward applies.
  The solutions depend on facts 2–3 above. If the engine changes how pulls are corrected, please re-run sim-w7.js.
